Here are similar examples translated into English, categorized the same way to teach children logical relationships:
Group 1: Reverse Relationships (Conversion)
(Rule: If X is [adjective] than Y, then Y is [opposite adjective] than X)
Speed: "The car is faster than the bicycle; therefore: The bicycle is slower than the car."
Heat: "Fire is hotter than ice; therefore: Ice is colder than fire."
Age: "Grandpa is older than Dad; therefore: Dad is younger than Grandpa."
Height: "The giraffe is taller than the horse; therefore: The horse is shorter than the giraffe."
Capacity: "The bucket is wider than the cup; therefore: The cup is narrower than the bucket."
Strength: "The lion is stronger than the tiger; therefore: The tiger is weaker than the lion."
Cleanliness: "This dress is cleaner than that one; therefore: That dress is dirtier than this one."
Fun: "Playing in the park is more fun than cleaning the room; therefore: Cleaning the room is less fun than playing in the park."
Sweetness: "Honey is sweeter than sugar; therefore: Sugar is less sweet than honey." (or "Sugar is not as sweet as honey")
Bravery: "Rami is braver than Samer; therefore: Samer is more scared (or less brave) than Rami."
Group 2: The Logical Chain (Transitive)
(Rule: If A > B, and B > C, then A > C)
Size (Animals): "The whale is bigger than the elephant, and the elephant is bigger than the dog.

Therefore: The whale is bigger than the dog.

(We also know): The dog is smaller than the whale."
Height (Buildings): "The tower is taller than the building, and the building is taller than the tree.

Therefore: The tower is taller than the tree."
Speed (Vehicles): "The plane is faster than the car, and the car is faster than the bike.

Therefore: The plane is faster than the bike."
Hardness (Materials): "The diamond is harder than iron, and iron is harder than wood.

Therefore: The diamond is harder than wood."
Age (Family): "My dad is older than my big brother, and my big brother is older than me.

Therefore: My dad is older than me."
Distance: "The street is farther than the park, and the park is farther than the school.

Therefore: The street is farther than the school."
Value: "The gold coin is more valuable than the silver coin, and the silver coin is more valuable than the copper coin.

Therefore: The gold coin is more valuable than the copper coin."
Temperature: "The sun is hotter than fire, and fire is hotter than a candle.

Therefore: The sun is hotter than a candle."
Group 3: The Opposite Chain (Using "but")
Just like your example: "The dog is bigger than the cat, but the cat is bigger than the mouse."
Height: "The tree is taller than the fence, but the fence is taller than the flower."
(Therefore: The tree is taller than the flower, and the flower is shorter than the tree).

Speed: "The cat is faster than the turtle, but the turtle is faster than the snail."
(Therefore: The cat is faster than the snail).

Age: "The big sister is older than her brother, but the brother is older than his little sister."
(Therefore: The big sister is older than the little sister).

Texture: "The wool is softer than the wood, but the wood is softer than the stone."
(Therefore: The wool is softer than the stone).

Sound: "The thunder is louder than the door, but the door is louder than a whisper."
(Therefore: The thunder is louder than a whisper).

Group 4: Challenge (Multiple Conclusions)
Mix both rules in one set of sentences.

In the Jungle:
"The gazelle is faster than the turtle.

The lion is faster than the gazelle.

Therefore: The lion is faster than the gazelle and the turtle.

And since the gazelle is faster than the turtle; the turtle is slower than the gazelle and the lion."
At the Playground:
"Sami is taller than Karim.

Karim is taller than Ali.

Therefore: Ali is shorter than Sami."
At the Toy Store:
"The doll is more expensive than the small car.

The small car is more expensive than the ball.

Therefore: The ball is cheaper than the doll."
I hope these help make the learning fun
