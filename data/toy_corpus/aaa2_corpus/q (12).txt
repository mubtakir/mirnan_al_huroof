The Approach for English Text:
Strategy 1: Direct Extraction (Easiest Method)
Convert clear facts into questions.

Original Text:
"Apple Inc. was founded by Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak on April 1, 1976, in Cupertino, California."
Training Examples:
Q: Who founded Apple Inc.?
 A: Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak.

Q: When was Apple Inc. founded?
 A: On April 1, 1976.

Q: Where was Apple Inc. founded?
 A: In Cupertino, California.

Strategy 2: Contextual Understanding
Create questions that require understanding relationships.

"Ancient Rome experienced periods of significant expansion and cultural prosperity. The Romans built excellent road networks that facilitated trade and military movement. They were also known for their advanced civil laws like the Twelve Tables."
Q: How did Roman roads impact the empire?
 A: They facilitated trade and military movement, aiding expansion and administration.

Q: Name two achievements of ancient Roman civilization.

 A: 1) Building excellent road networks. 2) Developing advanced civil laws like the Twelve Tables.

Example Application on a New English Text:
Original Text:
```
It was raining heavily that morning. Sarah decided to take the bus instead of walking to work. She waited at the bus stop for 15 minutes. When the bus arrived, it was completely full. Sarah decided to call a taxi instead. She arrived at work 30 minutes late but dry.

Diverse Training Examples:
Direct Questions:
Q: What was the weather like that morning?
 A: It was raining heavily.

Q: How did Sarah plan to go to work initially?
 A: She planned to walk to work.

Q: How long did Sarah wait at the bus stop?
 A: She waited for 15 minutes.

Q: Why didn't Sarah take the bus?
 A: Because it was completely full.

Sequential Understanding:
Q: What was Sarah's transportation sequence?
 A: 1) Plan to walk 2) Decide to take bus 3) Wait at bus stop 4) Find bus full 5) Take taxi.

Q: What happened between Sarah waiting at the bus stop and arriving at work?
 A: The bus arrived full, so she called a taxi.

Inference Questions:
Q: Why do you think Sarah took a taxi instead of waiting for another bus?
 A: She was probably running late and needed a guaranteed ride.

Q: How do you know Sarah avoided getting wet?
 A: Because the text says she arrived "dry" at work.

Cause and Effect:
Q: What caused Sarah to be late to work?
 A: The heavy rain and the full bus delayed her transportation.

Q: What was the result of Sarah taking a taxi?
 A: She arrived at work dry, though 30 minutes late.

Hypothetical Questions (for reasoning):
Q: What might have happened if the bus wasn't full?
 A: Sarah would have taken the bus and probably arrived earlier.

Q: If Sarah had brought an umbrella, could she have walked to work?
 A: Possibly, but the heavy rain might still have made walking difficult.

Summary Questions:
Q: What is the main problem Sarah faced?
 A: Transportation issues due to heavy rain.

Q: Summarize Sarah's morning in one sentence.

 A: Heavy rain forced Sarah to change her transportation plans multiple times, making her late but dry.

Question Type Templates for English:
WH-Questions:
What: "What did [subject] do?" / "What happened?"
Who: "Who [performed action]?"
When: "When did [event] happen?"
Where: "Where did [event] take place?"
Why: "Why did [subject] [action]?"
How: "How did [subject] [action]?"
Yes/No Questions:
"Did [subject] [action]?"
"Was/Were [subject] [state]?"
"Could [subject] [action]?"
Comparative Questions:
"What is the difference between X and Y?"
"Compare [concept A] with [concept B]."
Inference Questions:
"What can we infer about [subject]?"
"Based on the text, what probably happened next?"

Pro Tips for English Q/A Generation:
Vary question beginnings: Use different starters (What, How, Why, Describe, Explain, Compare, etc.)
Mix question types: Include factual, inferential, and analytical questions
Use synonyms: Rephrase concepts to test understanding beyond exact wording
Create multi-hop questions: Questions that require connecting information from different parts of the text
Include negative examples: "What did NOT happen?" or "Which statement is FALSE?"
